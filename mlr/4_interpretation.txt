1. Strongest spend-up drivers (statistically very strong and practically large)

Longer trips are the biggest lever: log_days_in_nz is very strong (p≈0).
Important nuance: this is a log predictor. A 10% longer stay implies about 4.9% higher spend, not 65% per extra day.
Package deals are a major uplift: about +31.4% spend (about +$1,073 at your baseline).
Mid-to-older age bands spend much more than the omitted age reference: many 40-69 groups are roughly +55% to +66%.
Premium trip style increases spend:
Luxury accommodation about +21%.
Top-end restaurants about +12.8%.
Rental car about +14.7%.
Some origin markets are strongly positive versus the reference country (for example US, England, Germany, Netherlands, Switzerland), many in +24% to +43% range.

2. Strongest spend-down drivers

Visiting friends/family motives reduce spending materially:
Visit family about -27.5%.
Wedding/funeral family occasion about -27.0%.
Visit friends about -23.9%.
Staying with family/friends reduces paid spend a lot: about -24.4%.
Maori_exp_none_of_these is strongly negative: about -16.5% (about -$566).
Some transport/accommodation/party indicators are also negative, but smaller magnitude.

3. What is clearly not a useful predictor

Gender is not important in your final model:
gender_male p=0.146
gender_other_or_unspecified p=0.981
Many long-tail activity and itinerary dummies have weak significance and tiny effect sizes. Good candidates to de-emphasize in narrative.

4. Storyline you can use in conclusions

High spend profile: longer stays, package travelers, older cohorts, premium accommodation/food choices, and some high-value source markets.
Lower spend profile: visitors whose purpose is mainly visiting family/friends and those staying with family/friends.
Cultural engagement signal: visitors selecting no Maori cultural experiences tend to spend less overall.

5. Two interpretation cautions (important)

Coefficients are associative, not causal.
For log_days_in_nz, do not use the file’s 65% figure as “per day effect.”
Use elasticity framing (for example 10% longer stay → about 4.9% more spend).
